# Power-ups: what the original has and what the mod does

Source of the list: the Crash Team Racing weapons and power-ups table on StrategyWiki (checked against the decomp's `HELD_ITEM_*` ids in `HeldItem`). A power-up has a normal
effect and a **juiced up** form, which a kart gets with 10 Wumpa fruit (`ItemManager.JUICED`). The roulette tables are in `ItemTables`; the design of the system is in
[DESIGN_ITEMS.md](DESIGN_ITEMS.md). Status: the Turbo, TNT/Nitro, Beaker and Bomb are built (`ItemManager.use`); the others are used up with a "not built yet" message.

| Power-up (`HeldItem`) | Normal effect | Juiced up | Notes for the mod |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tracking Missiles** (`missile`, `missile_3x`) | One or three. Home in on the closest kart ahead; a hit explodes the kart and stops it for a few moments. A warning target shows behind you when one is chasing you. Dodge by steering sharply or by releasing a rear-firing weapon. | Faster and track more precisely. | Needs kart targeting, a projectile entity, a warning cue. |
| **Bowling Bombs** (`bomb`, `bomb_3x`) | Fly in a straight line, thrown forward or backward; explode on an obstacle and stop karts in the blast radius. Can be detonated early with the use key again. | Bigger blast radius, easier to hit several karts. | Projectile entity; second press of the use key detonates. |
| **Power Shield** (`shield`) | Green shield protects once, for a few seconds, from any attack or obstacle, then disappears. Can be thrown forward like a bomb with the use key. | Turns blue and stays active until you are attacked or hit something dangerous. | A flag on the kart; thrown form is a bomb-like projectile. |
| **Explosive Crate / TNT** (`tnt`) | Dropped behind you. A kart that hits it is slowed, and it explodes after three seconds, stopping them. Can be got rid of by jumping repeatedly and quickly. | Becomes a Nitro crate: explodes at the slightest contact. | Look: the Minecraft TNT block as the base for the sprite and the placed entity, altered slightly so it reads as a power-up and not a real TNT block (decided with the author). |
| **N. Brio's Beaker** (`potion`) | Dropped green beaker; explodes on contact and stops the kart. Can be thrown forward by pressing the use key together with forward. | Turns red; also slows any kart that hits it for a few seconds. | Dropped/thrown entity. |
| **Turbo** (`turbo`) | A speed burst. | A bigger speed burst. | **Built**: fire level 128, 256 with ten Wumpa (`VehPickupItem_ShootNow`). |
| **Warp Orb** (`warpball`) | Reaches the kart in first place and stops it for a few moments. | Stops ALL karts in front of you. | Needs race order (`RaceManager.ranking`). |
| **Aku Aku / Uka Uka Mask** (`mask`) | Invincible for a few seconds; faster, invulnerable to attacks, hits opponents by contact. | Lasts longer. | Mask songs 1 and 2 of the level's song pack play while it is on (see [MUSIC.md](MUSIC.md)). |
| **N. Tropy Clock** (`clock`) | Briefly immobilises every kart but yours, then slows them for a few seconds. Very powerful, rare. | Effect lasts longer. | Rare in the tables. |
| **Super Engine** (`super_engine`) | **Battle Mode only**: more speed for a few seconds. | Lasts longer. | Only in the battle table (`BATTLE_DEFAULT`); not used by the race rule set. |
| **Invisibility** (`invisibility`) | **Battle Mode only**: invisible to others (not invulnerable) for a few seconds. | Lasts longer. | Only in the battle table. |

## Not in the original's list
`HeldItem.SPRING` (id 5) exists in the decomp's enum and no table draws it: cut content, kept only for the ids. It should not be offered in any rule set by default.

## Decisions so far
- Fidelity: the original's items and tables are the default rule set; anything else is a rule-set choice (see the fidelity policy in `CLAUDE.md`).
- The race roulette draws only the eleven items above (not Super Engine or Invisibility, which are battle-only).
- Kart-versus-Minecraft-entity effects of the weapons follow the entity-interaction policy in `CLAUDE.md` (on by default, tunable by config).
- Icons: the originals (extracted locally from the user's disc) are 48 x 32; the mod ships none and shows the power-up's name without a pack.

## Built: TNT and Nitro crate (2026-11-13)
`TntEntity` (+ `TntRenderer`): the use key drops the crate 1.9 blocks behind the kart (a Nitro crate when the kart has ten wumpa). The first other kart to touch it (the dropper is spared for 1.25 s) is slowed to a third of its class speed (`KartSim.itemSlow`) while the crate sits on its head and flashes, and three seconds later it explodes and spins that kart out (`itemBlast`); a Nitro crate explodes at the first touch. The explosion also throws and hurts other entities (vanilla explosion without block damage). The look is the Minecraft TNT block, 0.8 scale, flashing on a kart, green for Nitro. **Not done:** getting rid of the crate by jumping repeatedly, the wumpa lost by the victim, a crate hit by something other than a kart, the original's sounds and sprite for the crate.

### TNT details checked against the decomp (2026-11-13)
- **Slowdown:** none found. Nothing in the vehicle code reads the "TNT on head" pointer, damage type NONE does nothing, `RB_TNT.c` has no speed code. The manual's "slows you down" may be a feel from hopping (a kart in the air cannot accelerate), so the mod has none.
- **Jumps:** counted only once the crate has landed on the head (not during its flight onto it), one per jump that *starts* (`ACTION_JUMP_STARTED`). On landing `jumpsRemaining = 8 - (rng % 2)` with a signed rng: 9, 8 or 7 (a quarter, a half, a quarter); each counted jump uses one up and the next jump after zero throws the crate off, so 8, 9 or 10 jumps in the 90 frames (3 s) of the fuse. The mod counts them on the server (`TntEntity.hop`) the same way.
- **Safety frames:** the dropper is spared for 10 frames (`MINE_PARENT_SAFETY_FRAMES`); everybody else is hit at once, within 120 lu (2.4 blocks) of the crate.
- **Blasted animation:** `VehStuckProc_Tumble_Animate` plays frame `(time left / 32 ms) mod 27` of the table `Vector.c` generates (roll 2 turns, pitch swing up to 51 degrees, squash/stretch); ported in `KartRenderer`.

## Built: N. Brio's Beaker (2026-11-13)
`PotionEntity` (+ `PotionRenderer`: a 3D flask built in Blockbench, see below): the use key drops it behind the kart, or throws it forward in an arc when the forward key is held (`POTION_THROW_FORWARD`; speed 0x78 lu/frame, up 0x30, gravity 4 lu/frame, a wall bursts it, the ground lets it stay). Another kart than the dropper's (spared 10 frames) whose centre comes within 80 lu (1.6 blocks) spins out (`damage type SPIN`) and the beaker bursts with the glass sound (0x3F). A kart with ten wumpa throws a red beaker, which also puts a rain cloud over the victim for 7.68 s (`RB_RainCloud_Init`): item roll if it holds an item (the item is swapped for a new one when the cloud goes), else slow; a second red beaker restarts it with a random effect. **Applied:** item roll, icy ground, jump lockout. **Not yet:** the slow effect (the sim has no "damaged speed" yet), the rain cloud model and rain, heavy friction (the game's own cloud code never draws it), the beaker model and its flash effect, the pool limit of mines.

## The original's weapon models (2026-11-13)
They are in the shared model pack (`BIGFILE` entry 259, the same file as the icons): `crate_tnt`, `crate_nitro`, `crate_explosion`, `bomb`, `beakergreen`, `beakerred`, `warpball`, `cloud`, `shockwavered`, `shockwavegreen`, `shield`, `shielddark`, `shield_green`, `warpedburst`, `akumouth`, `akubeam`, `token`; the missile is not among the names found. `tools/ctr_disc_textures.py` `pack_models` lists them; `tools/export_ctr_mesh.py --pack` writes OBJ files to `local-assets/ctr-mesh/` (local only). The models with a single frame (the crates, bomb, shield, akubeam, token) export; the ones with compressed animation frames (the beakers, warp orb, cloud, shockwaves, warped burst, akumouth) are skipped until the frame deltas are decoded.
`tools/render_ctr_model.py` is a small software renderer (textures, palettes, vertex shading) that draws any of them from any angle. The TNT and Nitro crates are built from it: the pack makes a one-block cube with the model's own side, top and bottom pictures (`extract_ctr_textures.py`). For models that are not boxes the plan is directional sprites: pre-render a ring of views (a few pitches) and let the renderer pick the one that matches the angle the object is seen from, as the Wumpa fruit's spin does.
The weapon crate (`crate_question`) and the fruit crate (`crate_fruit`) are built the same way since 2026-11-13: the real models of Crash Cove's level file, rendered from the side, top and bottom with the game's vertex shading, one cube per pack with those pictures.

## Placed weapons: the pool and their life (2026-11-13)
- **Original** (`RB_MinePool.c`): one pool of **10** mines (TNT, Nitro, beakers, from every driver) for the race; a new one when it is full makes the oldest burst; one that is hit, or lands on a head, leaves the pool; nothing else times them out, they end with the race.
- **Mod** (`MinePool`): *in a race* one pool of 10 for the race, cleared when the race ends; *in free roam* a pool of 10 for each player, and a weapon nobody touches goes away after five minutes (`FREE_ROAM_LIFETIME_TICKS`). They are not saved with the world: unloaded chunks drop them.
- **Beakers:** green and red both spin the kart out (damage type SPIN, `VehPickState`), costing a wumpa; only an explosion (TNT, Nitro) is a blast with the tumble and the 3 wumpa. A red beaker adds the rain cloud a moment after the spin.
- **Hang time:** a blasted kart gets no hang-time boost from its bounces (`VehFire_Increment` refuses KS_SPINNING, KS_BLASTED and KS_MASK_GRABBED).

## Built: Bomb (2026-11-13)
`BombEntity` (+ `BombRenderer`: a billboard that shows one of 16 pictures of a full turn of the bomb about its axle, 22.5 degrees apart, chosen by the roll (67.5 degrees a game tick, as the original's `dir.x += 0x200`), in one of 8 columns chosen by where the camera is relative to the bomb's heading; tinted red when juiced). Port of `RB_MovingExplosive_ThTick/Explode`, `RB_Burst_Init` and the bomb branch of `VehPickupItem_ShootNow`. The use key rolls it ahead of the kart at 96 lu/frame, or backwards at 0.3 of that when the back key is held (`SHOOT_NOW_BACKWARD`; the use packet now carries the back key). It steers towards its target by 4/4096 turn per frame (`RB_Hazard_InterpolateValue`), climbs a block, and explodes on a wall, after 0x900 lu of fall without ground, on a kart within 96 lu (horizontally; the owner is spared for 60 frames and never hurt by the burst), on a mine, or when the owner presses the use key again (`VehPhysProc`: `instBombThrow`, `flags |= 2`). The burst blasts every other driven kart within 320 lu (512 lu with ten wumpa): blast tumble and the 3 wumpa penalty, sets off mines in reach, and a vanilla explosion of the same reach (no block damage) hurts and throws other entities. At most 12 bombs/missiles exist at once (`ACTIVE_MISSILE_LIMIT`); a launch beyond that keeps the item.
**Stand-ins, not in the decomp:** the target is the nearest other driven kart in a 45 degree cone up to 80 blocks ahead (the original tests the screen rectangle); a bomb is removed after 20 s; the launch/explosion sounds are the mine-drop and TNT ones. **Not done:** `bomb_3x` (still "not built"), the rolling loop (0x48), the burst's original look, bombs hitting missiles.
**The picture:** `textures/entity/bomb_roll.png`, an atlas of 8 columns (camera azimuth, 0 = from behind, 4 = from in front) by 16 rows (roll) of 64 px. The jar's is a stand-in drawn in 3D by `tools/make_bomb_sprites.py`; `tools/extract_ctr_textures.py` renders the original bomb (`render_ctr_model.roll_atlas`: the model turned about its x axis, the axle, seen from above at 20 degrees, one scale for all) into the local packs, which replace it. Model axes (from `mesh_vertices` and the game's matrices): x across the kart (the axle the bomb rolls about), y the way it faces, z up. The model is a ball of about 64 lu with a fuse that leaves it diagonally, half along the axle and half forward, reaching 94 lu out to a flat spark, so it sweeps a cone round the axle as the bomb rolls. The frame covers that reach, so the quad is 2.4 blocks wide with the ball about a third of it.

### Bomb, second pass (2026-11-13, from playing it and the original's footage)
- **Follows the terrain:** the original puts the bomb on the ground under it every frame and only a wall explodes it. The mod: after the horizontal move it steps down up to a block while on the ground (so it follows stairs, slabs and ramps down) and climbs a block up (`maxUpStep`); it falls when the ground ends and explodes after 46 blocks of fall or on a wall. (It uses the game's block collision, not the kart sim's ground query.)
- **Breaks crates:** `RB_Hazard_CollLevInst` returns 0 for fruit and weapon crates, so the bomb goes on, and the crate's own collide callback treats a bomb like a kart for the firing driver: the wumpa, or the roulette if the driver holds nothing. `PickupEntity.breakBy`.
- **The burst:** the vanilla explosion's look is gone (its entity damage and knockback are kept, driven by the config's `entities` section: damage, damagePlayers, damageScale, contact). The effect is `ModParticles.BURST`: the spiky burst picture swelling and fading yellow to red. The picture is the shared pack's `poweredup` icon (a quarter burst; mirrored four times), local pack only; the jar has a drawn stand-in. The original explosion's model (`warpedburst`, animated) is not decoded, so this is an approximation of its look.
- **Sounds:** `bomb_launch` (0x47), `bomb_roll` (0x48, a loop that follows the bomb), `bomb_explode` (0x49): drawn/synthesised placeholders in the jar, the originals through `tools/extract_ctr_sounds.py`.
- **HUD:** with ten wumpa a shining spiky burst sits behind the fruit and the held power-up (`UI_Weapon_DrawBG`: two layers, the second at 0xd000/0x10000, tinted by a sine); and a racer hurt by a spin or blast has their portrait in the race HUD drawn red for 30 frames (`damageColorTimer = 0x1e`; `HitFlashPayload`).
- **Speed and roll, checked against the decomp, not changed:** the bomb's 96 lu/frame is `sin * 3 >> 7`, and the roll `dir.x += 0x200` is 45 degrees a frame; the mod's kart tops out at 13140 / 256 = 51 lu/frame, so the bomb is 1.9 times a kart. Both are the decomp's numbers.

### Bomb, third pass (2026-11-13)
- **Ground:** the bomb now rolls on the kart's own terrain field (`TerrainField`: the cone surface, so a full block, a stair, a slab or a Copycats+ 45 degree wedge is a ramp), not on Minecraft's collision. Each substep (ten a game tick, 0.29 block) it puts itself on the surface under it, falls where the ground drops by more than a block, and explodes on a wall (`TerrainField.deepestWall`, a collision box the surface does not cover, tested with a 0.35 block sphere) or after 46 blocks of fall. The window is rebuilt every block and a half or 0.4 block of height change. The first version used vanilla collision and a one-block step-up, which a wedge's thin stepped collision defeated.
- **32 roll frames:** the atlas is 8 columns by 32 rows of 64 px (11.25 degrees a row; a game tick is six rows).
- **The shine** (`textures/gui/glow.png`) is coloured by brightness, pale yellow in the middle, orange, red-brown at the tips, as in the original's footage, and drawn untinted with a light-adding blend and linear filtering. The colour ramp is ours (`make_burst_sprites.ramp`): the game's own colouring of the `poweredup` picture is not in the code that was read, so the colours are matched to footage.

### Bomb, fourth pass (2026-11-13)
- **Wedges, dead on:** the bomb rides `TerrainField.surface`, which is smoothed and sampled at half-block cells, so on a steep slope it sits up to about half a block under the real crest. The wall test (a box 0.6 above it) took that crest for a wall, so a slope was climbed from an angle (the crest slips past) and not head on. A wall is now a box above the field's own step-up (1.25 blocks, `TerrainField.blocked`), which is what the kart climbs. `BombRollTest` rolls a bomb up Copycats+-style slopes (8 subdivisions a block in the mod's own shapes; tested from 1 to 32 slices, off-grid too, both ways), full blocks and slab stairs, and into a two-block wall.
- **The shine, exactly (`UI_WeaponBG_AnimateShine` / `UI_WeaponBG_DrawShine`):** the picture is the shared pack's `poweredup` icon, a QUARTER burst (centre in the bottom right corner), drawn four times mirrored as a gouraud quad: bright colour at the centre corner, middle colour at the two side corners, dark at the far corner. Two primitives, both with every texel semi-transparent (bit 15 set on all of them: `Vram.texture_stp`): a grey (0x5f..0xbe, off all channels, subtractive) and the colours (additive): bright (Y, Y, 0), middle (Y, G, 0), dark (R, g, 0) with Y = 0x7f..0xfe, G = 0x32..0x64, R = 0x21..0x42, g = 0x10..0x20, all rising and falling with |sin|. The ordering table draws the later primitive first, so the grey comes off first (clamped at black) and the colour is added to that: vivid, not a pale sum. `ItemHud.glow` does exactly that, with each pass drawn twice (a PS1 modulates by colour / 128, up to twice as bright; a GL colour is colour / 255, and two additions are the same). The pulse period (about 0.27 s) is a guess from the angle step, the size is ours.
- **Sounds carry further:** the roll loop has volume 3 (a variable-range sound carries 16 blocks times its volume), the launch 2.5, the explosion was already 4.

### Bomb, fifth pass (2026-11-13): the shine again, and the packs
- **Why it looked pale and spiky:** the `poweredup` picture's grey runs 0 to 189, dark at the rays and bright in the middle. The PS1 modulates by colour / 128, so its centre goes to full yellow; the first version doubled the texture (clamped at 255) and modulated by colour / 255, which throws that range away, and the rim, being dark, was all that stayed visible. Now the texture is the original's untouched and each pass is drawn twice. Simulated on a blue and a purple sky it is a vivid yellow blob with an orange fringe and dark red-brown rays; only the bottom of the pulse (|sin| near 0) is muddy.
- **Lines:** the texture wrapped round at its edges (the default), which drew the bright centre column along the far edges. `glow.png.mcmeta` has `clamp` now, with `blur`.
- **Packs:** `CTRCraft-OriginalTextures-OriginalScale` was missing the bomb atlas, the shine and the particle burst (they were only in the first pack's file list); both packs take them from one shared dict (`bomb_files`) now.

### Bomb, sixth pass (2026-11-13): the icons, the shine's range and shape, the use flicker
- **Icons keep their black:** the power-up icons have two near-blacks. Black with the semi-transparency bit (bit 15; `Vram.texture_stp`) is drawn half and half with what is behind (`DecalHUD_DrawWeapon(.., TRANS_50_DECAL)`): outlines, shading and a half-transparent backing square. An unflagged near-black such as (0, 0, 8) is opaque and is the backdrop around the picture. The first extraction flood-filled every near-black texel that touched the edge, which took the outlines and the shading with the backdrop. Now only the unflagged backdrop is removed (`power_icon`), and every flagged texel is kept at alpha 128. Texel 0x0000 is not drawn, as in the game; the originals are not alpha blended in any other sense.
- **The shine's pulse:** the game steps the phase twice a frame (each of the two draw calls adds 0x100), so its |sin| runs through its range in about four frames and reads as a mild flicker; the first version swung the whole range slowly. It uses 0.35 to 0.75 of the range now (`SHINE_LOW`, `SHINE_HIGH` in `ItemHud`), at the same 0.27 s period. Both the range and the period are matched to the footage by eye.
- **The shine's height:** wide as before; the fruit's is 22 reference pixels high (its spikes about reach the fruit's top and bottom) and the item's 1.3 times its box (they only just go past it).
- **Using an item:** pressing fire sets `noItemTimer` to 0x1e (30 frames); the weapon fires at once but the icon, with its box, stays and is drawn only every other frame until the timer is 0 and the item is taken away (`VehPhysProc.c`, `UI_Weapon_DrawSelf`). The HUD keeps the last held item for a second after the server clears it (`ItemHud.lastHeld`) and draws it every other 33 ms. The shine behind it stays. Not shown when the roulette starts instead (a rain cloud swapping the item).

### Bomb, seventh pass (2026-11-13): the icon's frame, the shine's size
- **Icon frame:** the previous pass still deleted part of the bomb's own outline, because the fill that removed the backdrop spread from the frame into the picture wherever the two touch. The icons have a one texel frame of unflagged near-black at the same place in all of them (rows 2 and h - 4, columns 3 and w - 3 as stored); only those four lines are cleared now (`power_icon`), and only where they really are mostly frame. Everything inside the frame, the picture's near-black shading and outlines included, stays, and the flagged black is kept at half alpha. The texel counts of the bomb: 298 transparent (0x0000), 275 half, 86 frame removed, the rest solid.
- **The shine's size** is measured on the original's footage and checked by drawing the real picture at the candidate size beside it (the valleys of the burst, 0.72 to 0.78 of its half height along the top, fall on the item frame's outline; the tips, 0.97, reach about a quarter of a frame past it): the item's burst is 2.0 times the frame's height (the width stays 2.3 times its width), the fruit's is 30 reference rows high, centred at 25.5 (the sprite's anchor is 23: the leaves sit on top, so the body is lower), the width 64 as before.

### Bomb, eighth pass (2026-11-13): the icons' black, separated properly
Earlier passes kept the icons' half-transparent black "because the game draws it", which left a dark rectangle behind every sprite. The black was never separated from the art. Looking at every texel of every icon (`Vram.texture_stp`): the texels with the semi-transparency bit are all pure black and form the icon's backing rectangle (in the pieces the art leaves visible: two large pieces for the bomb, only the four corners for the shield, which fills the rectangle), and a frame of one or two unflagged near-black texels runs round that rectangle at the same place in every icon. The art's own outlines and shading are unflagged near-black and lie inside the frame. `power_icon` now removes every flagged texel and the frame (straight runs of four or more in the frame band, so the art's outlines are never taken), and nothing else. Checked on white and on blue for the bomb, bomb tracker, 3 bombs, turbo, TNT, Nitro, beaker, shield, mask, clock and warp orb: no halo, no fragments of the frame, outlines intact.

### Beaker model and the rider's wheelie (2026-11-13)
- **The beaker is a 3D model now**, not the tinted vanilla potion bottle: `blockbench/pickups/beaker_green.bbmodel` and `beaker_red.bbmodel` (a round-bottomed flask from boxes: bulb, neck, lip; the sources, written once by `tools/make_beaker_models.py` and yours to edit), which generates `models/item/pickup_beaker_*.json` and draws the placeholder `textures/item/beaker.png` once (palette cells: liquid, shade, light, glass, for green and red). `PotionRenderer` draws the baked model: it turns slowly, lies on the slope it rests on (`TiltRender`), and pops in from half size over four frames as the original does (`RB_GenericMine_ThTick`: scale 0x800, +0x200 a frame to 0x1000).
- **Why a model and not sprites:** a beaker is seen from every side, tilted on slopes and thrown, which a sprite atlas cannot give without hundreds of pictures; a model costs one mesh. **The original's 15 frame "bubble" animation is not drawn** (decided with the author): the original's beaker is 40 triangles, the animation is stored delta-compressed, and a pack could still replace the model with the original's mesh (frame 0, as an OBJ through NeoForge's `neoforge:obj` loader) without the animation.
- **The rider follows the boost wheelie:** the player's model got the kart's ground tilt and blast tumble, but not the rear-up of a boost (lift, forward shift, nose-up pitch, squash and stretch). `KartRenderer.applyBodyTurn` applies the wheelie to the rider in the kart's own order (tilt, wheelie, tumble).

### The original beaker as a 3D model (2026-11-13)
- **Decoded:** the beakers' animation frames are delta-compressed (`ModelAnim.ptrDeltaArray`), which is why they were skipped. `tools/ctr_disc_textures.py` decodes them now (`_compressed_vertices`, a port of `RenderBucket_UncompressAnimationFrame`): the frame's bytes after its 0x1C header are an MSB-first bit stream read through little endian words (`RenderBucket_GetSignedBits`); the model's delta array holds one temporal word a vertex (bits 6-8, 3-5, 0-2: the three field widths minus one; bits 25-31, 17-24, 9-16: three biases, the first doubled), and each component is a running signed byte sum (`sum + field + bias`, or the field alone for a width of 8). Check: the beaker's 47 vertices use 218 of the frame's 224 bits, the three components come out with the model's own scales (848, 1585, 848 and offsets -127, 0, -127: x and z centred, the height 0 to 98.7 units), and the mesh is the symmetric flask. All 15 frames decode. **The shared pack's models have their height in the second coordinate** (the level models' is the third).
- **The model:** `tools/ctr_mesh_pack.py` makes, for the green and the red beaker (frame 0, so the bubble is in its first position and not animated), an OBJ and one atlas texture per beaker (every triangle's picture in a cell of its own with the game's shading baked in, an edge pixel repeated round each) and a model JSON with `"loader": "neoforge:obj"` (`flip_v`, no culling, `shade_quads` off), written by `tools/extract_ctr_textures.py` into both local packs under the stand-in's names (`models/item/pickup_beaker_green.json`, `_red`), so the pack's model replaces the jar's Blockbench flask and `PotionRenderer` draws it unchanged. 108.8 units to the block as the crates: 0.91 blocks tall, 0.49 wide. Checked by rendering the OBJ and the atlas alone (what NeoForge will read) from several angles: the flask, its blue neck, white lip, the liquid in the bulb, red or green.
- **Not done:** the bubble animation (15 frames; they decode, and could be 15 models switched by tick, but are skipped as decided), not seen in the game.
- `export_ctr_mesh.py --pack` can now export the other animated pack models too (warp orb, cloud, shockwaves and so on), which were skipped before; their height axis is the second coordinate as well.

### Axes of the shared model pack, and the bomb and crate pictures redone (2026-11-13)
Found while exporting the beakers to `local-assets/ctr-mesh` (they are there now, with the other animated pack models; the exporter's `--pack` knows the axes below):
- **The pack's models have their height in the second coordinate** (the TNT crate runs 0 to 109 up it, the characters, the beakers and so on too; the beaker's scale array is 848, 1585, 848); only the level file's models have it in the third, which is what `render_view` and the exporter assumed for everything. `render_ctr_model.Prepared(.., up=1)` turns a pack model a quarter about x, `(x, y, z) -> (x, -z, y)`, and keeps `render_view`'s mirror; the exporters (`up=1`, `ctr_mesh_pack`) leave +Y as it is and flip x.
- **The game's frame is left handed.** Two independent checks: the TNT and Nitro lettering reads correctly on the sides with that mapping, and the bomb, which moves towards its model's -z, shows its spark (+x) on the left from behind, as in the original's footage. So yaw 0 is the camera behind the bomb.
- **What this changed:** (1) the TNT and Nitro crate pictures: the "top" and "bottom" were side faces seen from the wrong axis; now the sides read upright (the model carries the lettering up its sides, the side picture is turned a quarter) and the top and bottom are the real ones. (2) The bomb's rolling atlas: its camera ring went round the model's forward axis, not the vertical one, and its fuse began pointing forward, not up. Now the ring is round the vertical axis, the pitch is from above, and the roll starts at 270 degrees (`roll_atlas(phase=270)`): from behind the skull faces the camera and the spark is at the top left, which is how the footage shows a fired bomb. `BombRenderer`'s column formula was already right for this. The stand-in atlas (`make_bomb_sprites.py`) starts with its fuse up and to the side the same way.
- Not affected: the beakers' model (it was built on the right axis from the start), the level models (fruit, "?" crate), the icons.

### Beaker lighting in the game (2026-11-13)
Seen in the game: some of the flask's triangles were lit oddly (bright and dark wedges on the cone and the lip), clean in Blender. Cause: `ObjModel.makeQuad` sets each quad's direction to the axis nearest its normal (`Direction.getNearest`), and the entity renderer lights a quad by that one direction, so the slanted triangles of one face landed on different axes. The OBJ now gives every face the normal (0, 1, 0), so all quads are lit the same and the game's own shading, baked into the atlas, is what shows (`ctr_mesh_pack`). Any OBJ model for a pack should do the same. Tested with a shader pack: no other problem seen.

### The beaker's bubbles (2026-11-13)
- **What the animation is** (all 15 frames decoded and compared): six of the beaker's forty triangles move, the other thirty-four never do. The six are bubbles, each a triangle whose picture (cut by its own uv triangle) is a round bubble, three dark and three bright and glossy, about 0.06 to 0.08 blocks wide at the original's 108.8 units to the block, rising from near the liquid (y about 0.15 blocks) to the lip (about 0.8) over the 15 frames and starting over, each at its own phase, with a sideways drift of a few hundredths of a block.
- **How they are drawn:** as billboards (`BeakerBubbles`, `PotionRenderer.renderBubbles`), not as part of the OBJ: the OBJ has the 34 static triangles only (the frozen speck above the flask is gone). The data is `assets/ctrcraft/beaker/beaker_<green|red>_bubbles.json` (frames, the sheet's texture, and per bubble its uv, size and the middle of it in every frame, in blocks, x and z from the flask's middle) and the sheet `textures/entity/beaker_<colour>_bubbles.png`. The local pack's, written by `ctr_mesh_pack` from the decoded frames, are the original's; the jar has a generic version (`tools/make_beaker_models.py`, written once, yours to edit: six bubbles climbing on a smooth curve) so the Blockbench flask bubbles too. The renderer is at the frame the game would be on (1.5 frames a tick from the flask's birth), blends between frames but not across the wrap, and the bubbles turn, tilt and pop in with the flask.
- The data is read again whenever resources reload (a reload listener), so a pack can be switched on and off in the game.

### The beaker is see-through, and shaded per vertex (2026-11-13)
Seen in the game: no bubbles, and the odd triangle shading again. Two causes, both in the data:
- **Every texel of every beaker texture has the semi-transparency bit** (all 40 triangles): the original draws the whole flask blended, half and half (10 triangles) or added (24, and the bubbles), which is why the bubbles are seen through the glass. The OBJ was opaque and hid them. Now the atlas carries an alpha per texel from the triangle's blend mode (128 for half and half, 176 as an approximation of added light), the renderer draws the bubbles first and the flask after them with `RenderType.entityTranslucent`, and the jar's stand-in flask has see-through glass and liquid too (`beaker.png`).
- **The shading is per vertex, not per triangle:** each vertex has its own colour from the model's colour table (`mesh_vertex_colours`; the corners of one triangle differ, for example colours 0, 10 and 9) and the game shades a textured triangle smoothly between them (Gouraud). The earlier bake used the one colour `mesh_triangles` reports for a triangle, which is what made the pink and white wedges; now `ctr_mesh_pack._bake` interpolates the three corners' colours across each triangle's cell. (The lighting fix of the normals stays: it is a separate thing, the quad direction.)
- `BeakerBubbles.load` logs "Beaker bubbles: green ..., red ..." at every resource reload: if the line says none, the data did not load.

### The bubbles' place, and the flask's faces (2026-11-13)
- **Bubbles stayed inside the flask:** `renderBubbles` took the bubble's place from `pose.last().pose()`, which already holds the flask's own place relative to the camera, and then translated by it again: every bubble was drawn at about twice the flask's distance along the same line of sight, so it looked as if it were inside the flask and never rose out of the top (the data does: the flask is 0.644 blocks tall and the bubbles rise to 0.79). The offset is now worked out on a stack of its own (tilt, spin, scale).
- **The flask's faces:** the translucent flask was drawn double sided, so the back faces showed through the front ones and added their own shade. It is `entityTranslucentCull` now, with the triangles wound outwards properly: `ctr_mesh_pack._outward` orients each connected piece from its shared edges (neighbours must run along their common edge in opposite directions) and turns the piece as a whole so its volume is positive (the flask is an open shell, so for a piece that is only approximate; a piece of fewer than four triangles falls back to the old test against the middle). Checked by drawing the OBJ with back faces culled: solid from the sides, an empty opening from above. The base is open (there is no bottom triangle), which is never seen.
- The OBJ's vertices are not shared between triangles (each triangle has its own three): that is harmless here, the shading is baked into the atlas and every face has the same normal, so no smoothing or lighting depends on shared vertices.

### The beaker's colour and winding (2026-11-13)
- **Winding:** the flask is a tube (open at the base; the top has the liquid's cap and the lip's opening), so its pieces are oriented by the radial direction (outwards is away from the vertical axis), not by a volume, which means nothing for an open surface. The lip, a flared collar, was turned inside out by the volume test; now all 34 triangles face outwards (`ctr_mesh_pack._outward`, checked per triangle).
- **No table shading:** the model's colour table, shaded per vertex, tinted the flask (brown and blue at the base and the neck: its entries 0 and 8) and the original's flask is not tinted like that (its HUD icon is a green flask with a pale neck), so by default the pictures are used as they are, plus their transparency (`SHADE_FROM_TABLE` in `ctr_mesh_pack` turns the Gouraud shading back on). The bubbles likewise: they were darkened to half by the table and are now as bright as the liquid.

### Flask scale, crate break-up, 3D Wumpa (2026-11-13)
- **Scale:** the pack's models are in blocks at 108.8 units to the block (a crate is exactly 1), so the original's flask is 0.87 of a crate tall. `PotionRenderer.SIZE` was 0.62 (it was fitted to the entity's 0.6 box); it is 1.0 now: the picture is in proportion to the crates, the box is only the hit box. `tools/export_ctr_mesh.py` now defaults to the same scale (it was 50 units a block, which made the crates 2.2 wide) so the Blender files can be compared directly; `--scale 0.02` gives the old one. The bomb's mesh is not warped in the data (its body is 76 units across on every axis; the flat ones, shields, warp orb, cloud, token, are flat in the game's data too).
- **Crate break-up** (`RB_CrateWeapon_ThCollide`, `RB_CrateFruit_ThCollide`, `RB_CrateAny_ThTick_Explode`): the solid crate is shrunk to nothing and an instance of STATIC_CRATE_EXPLOSION is born on it, its animation advancing one frame a game tick (12 frames, 22 triangles flying out and up), then dies. The weapon crate's is turned at random about the vertical axis; the instance also carries a colour (`colorRGBA`, a tint of the pieces) which is **not** applied here: the format of that word is not documented in the decomp. The model's height is its third coordinate (the pieces fly along it), unlike the shared pack's usual second. Its first frame is taken to be the crate's own place (the model's origin is far from the pieces). Done as a particle (`CrateBreakParticle`, data `crate_break/crate_break.json` + `textures/entity/crate_break.png`): the jar has a stand-in of 18 wood splinters (`tools/make_crate_break.py`), the local pack the original's pieces (`ctr_mesh_pack.shard_files`). The server sends entity event 77 just before it removes a taken crate (a kart touching it or a bomb); fruit crates and weapon crates only. The TNT / Nitro crates' own explosions (`RB_Explosion_InitGeneric`, the same model, red for TNT and green for the others) are not hooked up yet.
- **3D Wumpa** (local pack): the fruit of Crash Cove's level file as an OBJ with its Gouraud colours baked into an atlas (`ctr_mesh_pack.mesh_pack_files(..., up=2, animated=False, honour_cull=True)`); it replaces the animated sprite in `pickup_wumpa.json`, the strip stays for the HUD. The model's four triangles without the game's cull flag (the leaves) are written both ways round, and the pickup renderer draws without culling anyway. The beaker keeps its culling (`honour_cull` is off for it).
- **Normals:** every face of the pack OBJ models has the normal (0, 1, 0) (NeoForge's loader would pick a lighting direction from the real normals); the winding is what must be right, and `_outward` works it out for each connected piece from its shared edges (consistent) and then the sign (radial for a tube, volume otherwise).
